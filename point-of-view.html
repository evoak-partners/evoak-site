← Evoak Partners The point of view

The technology is ready. Most companies aren't.

It's easy to spend real money on AI and get nothing back. Here's how we think about the difference between the companies that pull ahead and the ones that just get busier.

Waiting is a decision too.

Waiting doesn't work. Neither does trying a little of everything. The field is moving fast, and the companies pulling ahead aren't the ones running the most experiments - they're the ones who picked a real change and moved on it while everyone else was still deciding.

Almost every company is using AI now. Very few are making real money from it.

That gap is where the next few years get decided. So pick where to move, and move soon.

It doesn't have to be big.

Being deliberate doesn't mean launching something enormous. The first move is often a small one - a single job done properly, start to finish, so you can watch it work and learn what it takes.

Some of the best first moves are hard to put a number on. What matters isn't the size of it. It's whether it's pointed at something that genuinely matters to your business, and whether you see it through.

The trap isn't the technology. It's how it gets bought.

The gains from everyday AI tools are real, and they feel like progress - which is exactly what makes the spending easy to start and hard to stop. Best case, you get what you paid for and you're no more different from your competitors than you were. The money goes out, everyone feels busier, and none of it reaches the bottom line.

Automate a bad process and all you've done is get faster at doing the wrong thing.

What kind of business do you run?

Is it really one repeatable task wearing a costume? Or is it built on judgment, relationships, and someone being accountable when it counts?

The first kind gets replaced. The second kind, run well, comes out further ahead - and most established companies are the second kind, without yet knowing how to press the advantage.

It will ask something of you.

This isn't something you can hand to everyone and hope. Electricity took decades to change how factories ran. The PC took more than ten years. Both needed people who knew what they were doing.

Your team can tell you what's broken better than anyone, so listen to them - but knowing what's broken isn't the same as knowing how to fix it. Buying AI off the shelf and pointing it at the work fails even at the best companies. Whether it holds up has little to do with which AI you bought, and everything to do with how the whole thing was built.

It asks new skills of you, too, and a new way of looking at your own business and the market you're in. That part doesn't come out of a box.

Somebody's going to make this pay.

The only question is who. Our job is to make sure it's you - on purpose, and at a pace you set.
